description: "Brain age prediction has become a widely used machine learning framework to study healthy aging, neurological disorders, and individual differences across the lifespan. However, progress in this field has been possible largely thanks to the principles of open science: openly shared datasets, transparent methodologies, and collaborative software development. In this talk, I will present an overview of brain age modelling from an open science perspective, discussing how machine learning approaches can leverage large-scale neuroimaging datasets to estimate biological brain aging. Using examples from my own research, I will illustrate how open datasets, reproducible analysis pipelines, and open-source software have been essential for developing, validating, and disseminating brain age models. Beyond the scientific results, I will share my experience building research around free and open-source software ecosystems, contributing to community-driven projects, and adopting reproducible computational practices.<br /><br />
<strong> About </strong><br />
Asier Erramuzpe Aliaga is an Ikerbasque Research Fellow at the Computational Neuroimaging Lab at Biobizkaia. His research focuses on computational neuroscience and biomedical engineering. He holds a PhD in Biomedical Research from the University of the Basque Country and has previous research experience at Mondragon Unibertsitatea, the Hebrew University of Jerusalem and Freiburg University Hospital.<br /><br />
